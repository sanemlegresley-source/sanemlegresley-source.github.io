Foil right-of-way trainer 🤺

A foilist's aside. In foil both fencers can land at once and only one light counts — the referee decides who had right of way. It's the rule newcomers find maddening and the reason foil looks like an argument with swords. So: read the exchange, call the touch, and see if the referee agrees.

What even counts — the target

Foil is a torso-only weapon. Hit an arm, leg, or the mask and it simply doesn't score (in épée it's the whole body; in sabre, everything above the waist).

Valid target — the trunk, front and back, from the collar to the groin lines. This is the area covered by the metallic lamé.

Off target — head, arms, and legs. A hit here stops nothing and scores nothing.

Because the target is small and shared, foil rewards a clean, deliberate phrase over a fast flail — which is exactly what makes right of way the heart of the game.

A little French

Attaque

The initial offensive action — extend and threaten. Whoever starts one correctly first holds priority.

Parade & riposte

Deflect the incoming blade, then answer. A found parry hands priority to the defender.

Contre-attaque

Hitting into the attack without parrying first. Brave, but it has no right of way.

Pointe en ligne

Point-in-line: arm straight, point on target, established early. Must be removed before attacking.

Remise

Re-hitting after your attack was parried or missed — usually loses to a proper riposte.

Simultané & touché

Same-tempo attacks (no touch, replay); and the classic acknowledgement of a hit landed.
