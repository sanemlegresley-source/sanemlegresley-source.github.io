Designing an agentic CX concierge, end to end

A case study taking an agentic customer experience from discovery to hand-off for a confidential premium retail/CPG brand. It shows the craft end-to-end, the product thinking and the experience-design deliverables and how agentic AI reshapes both. Client details are anonymised under NDA; the specifics shown are representative, the method and thinking are the real thing.

Approach · Design thinking

The lens: human-centered design and what I take from my friends at IDEO

My grounding is in design thinking. I've been lucky to learn a great deal from friends and mentors at IDEO and that human-centered instinct shaped every decision on this project. Concretely, here is where it showed up.

Start from empathy, not features

I began with the customer's emotional state, not a spec. The personas and the confidence curve came before any screen, because the real problem was an anxiety ("will this be right for me?") not a missing feature.

Reframe with "How might we"

The brief said "add an agent." I reframed it as "how might we make Maya confident enough to buy without fear of a return?" That reframing turned the agent into a confidence layer in the IA rather than a deflection tool.

Desirability, feasibility, viability

IDEO's three lenses run through the PRD: desirability (customer confidence and trust guardrails), feasibility (grounded answers, clean hand-offs, a design system to build against) and viability (revenue per session, a monetisable offering). A solution has to clear all three.

Build to think

The wireframes are deliberately greyscale and rough. Prototyping low-fidelity keeps the conversation on structure and lets us kill weak ideas cheaply, long before anyone falls in love with a pixel.

Test and let evidence win

Acceptance criteria are written as hypotheses a QA can actually run and the holdout keeps the readout honest. Treating "flat" as a valid, useful outcome is design-thinking humility: you learn as much from what didn't move.

Radical collaboration

The accessibility annotations and the technical hand-off exist because durable experiences are built with engineering, not thrown over a wall. Design thinking is a team sport and the artefacts are built to travel.

01 · Discovery

The brief and what a quick audit surfaced

Ask: The brand's e-commerce converts well on intent-led visits but leaks badly everywhere else, fit uncertainty, product overload and a support queue that treats pre-sale questions like tickets. They want an agentic concierge that guides shoppers, resolves post-purchase issues and does it in the brand's voice.

My frame: don't bolt a chatbot onto the funnel. Treat the agent as a new layer of the experience with its own IA, guardrails and hand-offs, measured on outcomes (fit confidence, deflection, revenue per session), not on containment.

Site-audit findings (heuristic + analytics)

  • Fit & sizing answers buried 3 taps deep; 22% of PDP exits touch the size chart first.
  • Search returns 180+ results for "rain jacket" with no guided narrowing.
  • Post-purchase help is a generic form; median first response > 8 hours.
  • No consistent component library, 4 button styles across 6 templates.
  • Strong brand photography and a loyal, values-driven customer base to build on.

02 · Who we're designing for

Three personas, one shared anxiety: "will this be right for me?"

Maya · The considered first-timer

New to technical gear · high research, low confidence

Goal

One jacket that's genuinely right for wet coastal hikes.

Frustration

Jargon (GORE-TEX vs. membranes), sizing dread, fear of returns.

Wants from an agent

Plain-language guidance and a confident "this one, this size."

Dev · The loyal replenisher

Owns the brand · time-poor, knows what he likes

Goal

Re-buy or upgrade fast, without re-deciding everything.

Frustration

Re-navigating the catalogue; unclear what's changed model-to-model.

Wants from an agent

"Same fit, newer model" and a one-tap reorder.

Priya · The post-purchase problem-solver

Recent buyer · something's wrong, patience thinning

Goal

Resolve a warranty/return question without a ticket black hole.

Frustration

Repeating context; slow, templated replies.

Wants from an agent

Immediate, accurate resolution and a human when it matters.

04 · Information architecture

Where the agent lives in the IA

The concierge is a persistent layer, not a buried page, reachable from anywhere, with its own sub-flows and a clean CMS-authored knowledge source.

  • Home
  • ├─ Shop (category → subcategory → PDP)
  • ├─ Guides & Fit (CMS-authored, agent-readable)
  • ├─ Account (orders, returns, warranty)
  • └─ Concierge (persistent layer)
    • ├─ Guided shopping → fit finder → recommendation
    • ├─ Reorder & upgrade → "same fit, newer model"
    • ├─ Support & warranty → resolve → human hand-off
    • └─ Knowledge source = Guides + PDP data + policy (single source of truth)

06 · Product requirements

PRD, in brief

Problem

High-intent shoppers convert; everyone else churns on uncertainty. Support treats pre-sale questions like tickets, adding cost and friction.

Goals

  • Lift assisted-session conversion and average order value.
  • Cut fit-related returns via confident, explained sizing.
  • Deflect routine support with accurate self-resolution + clean hand-off.

Non-goals

  • Replacing human support for complex or emotional cases.
  • A general-purpose chatbot untethered from brand & policy.
  • Dark-pattern upselling that erodes trust.

07 · Stories & acceptance criteria

Ticket-ready, with acceptance criteria a QA can run

Given/When/Then keeps intent, edge cases and the hand-off explicit, so estimates hold and nothing is "implied".

Story: guided fit recommendation

# As Maya, I want a confident size call so I don't fear a return.
Scenario: Agent recommends a size
  Given I've shared my usual size and use case
  When I ask "what size should I get?"
  Then the agent names one size
  And gives a one-line reason grounded in fit data
  And states the return window before I add to cart

Scenario: Low confidence
  Given the fit data is ambiguous
  When I ask for a size
  Then the agent says it isn't sure
  And offers two options with the trade-off
  And never fabricates a measurement

Story: support resolution & hand-off

# As Priya, I want a fast, accurate answer, or a human.
Scenario: Resolves within policy
  Given my question is covered by policy
  When I ask about a warranty claim
  Then the agent answers from the policy source
  And cites what it's basing that on
  And logs the interaction to my account

Scenario: Escalation with context
  Given the case is out of policy or emotional
  When resolution isn't possible
  Then the agent routes me to a human
  And passes the full transcript + order context
  And tells me what happens next and when

11 · Point of view

What agentic AI changes about this discipline

Agentic AI doesn't retire experience design, it moves the work up a layer. When an agent can generate a screen, the scarce skill is no longer producing artefacts; it's deciding what's right: the IA the agent reasons over, the guardrails that keep it on-brand and on-policy, the hand-offs that protect trust and the metrics that separate a helpful experience from a containment tactic. The design system becomes the substrate the AI generates against, which is exactly why component libraries and accessibility stop being hygiene and become leverage.

The economics shift too. If AI absorbs the repetitive, a team's value concentrates in judgement, taste and rigour, the discovery that prevents rework, the acceptance criteria that hold an estimate, the audit that catches a margin leak before it ships. My take: the durable experiences won't be the ones that added an agent, but the ones that redesigned the journey around one, with a clear point of view about where a human should still show up. That's the work I want to lead.
