01 · The operating model

One function, four services, measured on outcomes

I treat operations as a product, not a cost centre. Each service has an owner, a small set of measures that matter and a clear promise to the business. The promise is the point: an availability number only means something when it maps to work a person was able to finish.

Cloud & infrastructure

Compute, network, data platforms and the cloud landing zones underneath them. Run on infrastructure as code, with capacity, resilience and cost designed in rather than bolted on.

Digital workplace

Endpoints, identity, collaboration and the everyday tools people work in. The experience employees have with their own technology is a business enabler, not a back-office chore.

Service Desk & service management

The front door and the discipline behind it: incident, request, problem and change, run on ITIL-aligned practice without the ceremony that slows good teams down.

Disaster recovery & resilience

Recovery that is tested, not assumed. Tiered recovery objectives, regular failover drills and the evidence a regulator and a board both want to see.

02 · Reliability & error budgets

How much downtime a target actually buys

A service-level objective is a promise with a budget attached. The gap between one hundred percent and the target is the error budget: the downtime you are allowed to spend before you stop shipping change and start shoring up reliability. 03 · Incident management

Detect fast, restore faster, learn without blame

Incidents are inevitable, slow recovery is not. The goal is to find them before a person reports them and to restore service before the business feels it. Blameless reviews turn each one into a fix that stops the next.

Mean time to restore, trailing eight quarters

Minutes, Sev-1 and Sev-2 combined. The fall is observability and runbooks doing their job.

Detect

Service-level alerting on what users feel, not on every raw metric. Fewer, better pages.

Respond

A clear incident commander role, defined severities and one bridge. Communication is a first-class task.

Learn

A blameless review within days, with action items that have owners and dates and get checked.

04 · Change & modern engineering

Moving fast without breaking trust

Speed and safety are not opposites when change is small, automated and reversible. The slowest way to run a regulated platform is a once-a-quarter big-bang release. The safest is many small ones that are easy to reason about and easy to undo.

Infrastructure as code

Environments are defined, reviewed and version-controlled. No snowflake servers and no undocumented changes made at midnight.

Progressive delivery

Automated pipelines, canary and staged rollouts and fast rollback. Risk is reduced by design rather than by a long approval queue.

Platform engineering

Paved paths and self-service golden templates so teams ship on guardrails. Standards become the easy option, not the enforced one.

05 · Disaster recovery & resilience

Recovery you have actually rehearsed

A recovery plan that has never been run is a hope, not a control. I tier services by how fast and how recent their recovery has to be, then prove each tier in scheduled failover drills that produce evidence for the board and the regulator.

06 · Service management & the Service Desk

07 · AIOps, observability & FinOps

Intelligence and cost discipline in the run

Modern operations run on signal and on spend. AI-enabled tooling cuts alert noise and catches the anomaly a human would miss at three in the morning and a FinOps habit keeps cloud cost honest while the platform grows.

Observability first

Logs, metrics and traces tied to the user journey, so a problem is visible as a degraded experience before it becomes an outage.

AIOps where it earns its keep

Correlation and anomaly detection to cut noise and group related alerts, plus automated remediation for the well-understood failures.

FinOps in the open

Cost allocated to owners, rightsizing and commitment coverage reviewed on a rhythm and spend treated as an engineering metric.

08 · Leading change in a stable environment

Challenging the status quo without destabilising it

The hardest version of this job is not a greenfield build, it is modernising a platform people already depend on while it stays up. That is a leadership problem before it is a technical one. It is done by bringing a team through change, by being technically credible enough to earn their trust and by making the safe path the fast one.

Credible, not distant

A team modernising hard systems follows a leader who understands the work. I stay close enough to the detail to make the real calls.

Change people can absorb

Transformation that lands is sequenced and explained. A team of around thirty can carry a lot of change when they can see the why and the next step.

Stability is the platform for speed

Reliability is not the opposite of ambition. A dependable platform is exactly what lets you move quickly and take the bets that matter.
