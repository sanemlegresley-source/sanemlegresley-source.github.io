Leadership · Building the team

The mission is the team, not the accounts

When the playbooks for a new multi-product motion are still being written, the leader's job is not to manage accounts. It is to build the team that does and to define what great looks like before anyone has. Here is how I build and develop that team.

Hire for consultative instinct

A high, intentional bar. I hire people who uncover a customer's real goals and ask the better question, not people who simply keep accounts warm.

Coach to consultative depth

I develop CSMs into partners who set accurate expectations, run change management and help customers rethink how they work when a deeply embedded tool is being displaced.

A scorecard everyone can see

A performance framework built on outcomes, account health, adoption, retention and expansion, with clear paths for recognition, development and remediation.

A predictable operating cadence

1:1s, account and forecast reviews, save reviews and enablement on a rhythm. Cadence is what turns good intentions into accountability.

Develop people on purpose

Direct feedback, real growth plans and the conditions for each CSM to do the best work of their career. Demanding and developmental are the same thing done well.

Experiment, then codify

Test new ways to deliver strategic CS, document what works and turn it into a repeatable motion that scales past the strategic tier. The team is where the standard gets set.

05 · Executive business review

The QBR that earns a seat at the table

A strategic review is not a usage report. It is a business conversation the sponsor wants on their calendar. The frame I coach CSMs to run:

Look back

Outcomes against the goals we set, in the sponsor's language and units. Adoption depth across the suite. What we learned together.

Look around

Benchmarks against peers, where value is leaking, the one or two risks we are actively managing and the honest health call.

Look forward

Next quarter's goals, the roadmap that matters to them and a clear expansion business case with a mutual action plan.

Product partnership · Voice of customer

The team is the product's sharpest instrument

Strategic CSMs sit closest to the customers who matter most, so the team is the primary voice for what those customers need next. My job is to turn that into signal the product can act on.

Synthesise, don't relay

Account-level adoption, health and qualitative feedback become one clear, prioritised point of view, the pattern across the portfolio rather than the loudest anecdote.

Aim it at what's new

The newest AI surfaces need the sharpest feedback loop. I make sure what strategic customers hit first shapes the roadmap while it still can.

One unified experience

Partner across Sales, Solutions Engineering, Product and Partnerships so an account feels one company from pre-sale through expansion and so friction gets removed, not routed around.

07 · Point of view

How AI changes strategic customer success

AI does not replace the strategic CSM. It removes the reasons they were spread thin. Agentic monitoring watches every account's usage, sentiment and support signals continuously, so the human shows up already briefed instead of spending the week assembling a picture. Call sentiment becomes a live health input rather than a quarterly survey. Notes, follow-ups and QBR prep get drafted, so the CSM's scarce hours go to the conversation, the judgement and the relationship.

For a company whose product is AI productivity, the CS motion should be the proof case: run the team on the same leverage we sell. My take is that the winning strategic CS org will look less like a queue of accounts and more like a small number of trusted advisors, each amplified by agents, each spending their time exactly where a human still changes the outcome. That is the team I want to build.
