Digital citizen experience

Designing public services around people

A digital citizen experience engagement I delivered with a Fortune 500 consulting company for a public-sector client: modernising how citizens find and complete the services they need, built on a simple idea, that a service should be designed for people, not agencies. The goal was confidence in government, earned through experiences that are simple, human and secure. The client and firm are kept confidential, with some details changed to protect them.

01 · The mandate

Simple, human and secure

The real problem: citizens found the service hard to navigate, wrote in plan language they did not understand and fell back to the phone or the counter for tasks that should have been simple online. Low trust and high effort, on both sides of the desk.

The frame we used: design around how people actually live, not how government is organised. A simpler experience that still feels human, kept secure and reaches everyone, is how a public service earns confidence back.

The three principles we held to

  • Simple. Make it easy to get the outcome a citizen needs, in as few steps as possible.
  • Human. Balance digital and in-person and serve with empathy. Digital-first is not digital-only.
  • Secure. Protect people's data and identity, because trust is the whole point.

02 · Who we served

Citizens in the changing context of their lives

We designed around life events, the moments when people actually meet government and we designed for the public servants who serve them.

Newcomer

New to the country, new to the system

Needs to access services without knowing which agency owns what, often in a second language.

In transition

A job loss, a new child, a loss in the family

A stressful life event that touches several programs at once and cannot wait on a queue.

Everyday citizen

Digital-native, time-poor

Expects a renewal or an application to take minutes on a phone, or they disengage.

Public servant

Serving at the counter and the phone

Needs tools and confidence to help, not a system that fights them while a citizen waits.

04 · Organise around life

Journeys built on how people live, not how government is structured

We reorganised the experience around life events that cross agency boundaries, with the personal, secure work behind one trusted sign-in and a verified digital identity.

  • Start here (leads with the top life events)
  • ├─ Starting out (new to the country, first job, first home)
  • ├─ A change in life (job loss, new child, caring for someone)
  • ├─ Everyday services (renewals, applications, payments)
  • ├─ Get help (plain-language guidance, human support)
  • └─ My account (secure)
    • ├─ Verified digital identity
    • ├─ My applications & status
    • └─ My documents & notices

05 · Notices & forms

A letter a citizen can read once and act on

Government notices are where complexity does the most damage. We rewrote them as structured, plain-language content: a clear reason, a clear next step and a date, accessible by default and authored once so a policy change updates every notice cleanly.

Before

"Pursuant to the aforementioned application, the Ministry requires further substantiating documentation in order to adjudicate your eligibility for the benefit in question."

After

"We need one more document to finish your application. Please send proof of address by 15 March. Here is how and here is what happens next."

06 · Accessible & equitable

Equitable access and reach, by design

A public service has to work for everyone, which means accessibility is a design principle and in-person stays a first-class channel, not a fallback.

Designed in

  • WCAG 2.2 AA as the baseline for web, services, forms and notices.
  • Plain language and a readability target on every page.
  • Keyboard, screen-reader and reflow checks built into components.
  • Multilingual and low-literacy paths, not an afterthought.

Channels kept open

  • Digital-first, never digital-only. The counter and the phone stay for those who need them.
  • Assisted digital, so staff can help a citizen through the online path.
  • Consistency across channels, so the service feels like one government.
  • Accessibility measured and reported, not assumed.

07 · Design system & content

Quality that scales because it is built once

A design system and a shared content model let many teams across many agencies ship consistent, accessible services. Every component carried its accessibility and its plain-language patterns, so each new page started compliant.

Design system

Accessible, tested components and tokens. One pattern for a form, a status, an identity check, reused across services.

Content model

Structured, reusable content for guides, forms and notices, authored once and published everywhere, plain language baked in.

Governance

Clear standards, review and quality gates, so consistency, security and accessibility hold as the service grows.

08 · Responsible AI & identity

Technology deployed with intention

Digital tools earned a place only where they made a citizen's task easier or a public servant's work lighter, never at the cost of trust.

Digital identity: a verified, reusable credential in a secure vault, so a citizen proves who they are once and moves between services without starting over. Security and consent are the design, not a disclaimer.

Responsible AI: grounded assistance that answers from approved, plain-language policy and hands off to a person for anything sensitive or specific to a citizen's case. Behind the scenes, drafting and triage that free staff for the human work. Always reviewed, always governed.

09 · Delivered in phases

Phased delivery, with people carried through the change

A transformation this size succeeds in phases that each ship real value, with change management and workforce enablement so staff, partners and citizens adopt it.

Phase 1 · Foundations

  • • Design system and content model
  • • Top life-event journeys, rebuilt and accessible
  • • Plain-language standard for notices and forms

Phase 2 · Services

  • • Secure sign-in and verified digital identity
  • • Apply, track and renew online
  • • Notices modernised and connected

Phase 3 · Whole-of-government

  • • Services joined across agencies
  • • Grounded citizen assistance
  • • Proactive, personalised help
Change management: every phase shipped with enablement for frontline staff, clear communication to citizens, partner and vendor alignment and a read on adoption. Confidence in the workforce was treated as part of the outcome, not a side effect.
